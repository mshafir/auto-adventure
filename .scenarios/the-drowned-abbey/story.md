# The Drowned Abbey

An abbey on the sandflats goes under, and the news travels east faster than the water does.

## The beginning

The player arrives on the shore road south of Wenthollow, a ferry village of ten roofs whose
jetty is longer than its high street. The ferry is not running. Ilse Wentworth, who owns it,
will not cross to an abbey whose bell has not rung for two days — and the bell is the only
clock forty people have.

## The middle

East along the shore road is Ash Hollow, five houses in a dip of the woods around a well
nobody trusts. Rell Ashcombe keeps the shrine there, which was raised for the people the sea
kept, and has been up on the rise three times to look at the water over the spit. It has not
gone back.

While the player is standing at the well, a rider comes in through the western gate with the
news said out loud: the abbey is under, and the road behind them is full of people walking
inland.

## The end

That turns the chapter. By morning the millrace at Wenthollow is running with things that were
not in it yesterday, and one of them is a lead seal on a broken cord — the abbey's, still
bright where the cord protected it. It is the only thing left of the place.

## Who is in it

- **Ilse Wentworth**, ferryman at Wenthollow. Blunt, and not unkind about it. Talks about tides
  the way other people talk about weather. She is the reason the player goes east.
- **Bran Cawle**, miller, indoors at Wenthollow Mill. Talks a great deal, mostly about the
  millrace — which is where the seal turns up, so he is the reason the player looks there.
- **Rell Ashcombe**, shrine-keeper at Ash Hollow. Careful with words, waits to be asked twice.
  Knows what the water has done because they have been out looking at it.
- **Hesper Ashcombe**, netmender, who shares Rell's words. There so the hollow has more than
  one person in it.

## What each place is for

- **Wenthollow** is where the story starts and ends: the errand out, and the seal in the
  millrace after.
- **Ash Hollow** is where the news is confirmed and where it arrives out loud. The cutscene
  happens at its well because that is the one place in the hollow everybody passes.
- **The shore road between them** is authored — the generator gave both towns roads but not to
  each other — and it widens into a trodden verge in the second chapter, because everybody
  fleeing the abbey walked along it.
