# The Lantern Watch

A chain of hill beacons warns the coast of raiders. One did not light, and nobody will say why.

## The beginning

The player comes up the shore road carrying a **warden's tally** — a notched stick that says
whose turn it is to keep which beacon, and which is the only proof the rota was ever agreed.
They are bringing it back to Salt Wick because the season has turned and the watch changes
hands.

They arrive to find the village already out in the square. Two nights ago the beacon on
Cairnhead did not light, and the boats that should have been warned came home to find the
smokehouse robbed. Nobody in Salt Wick will say whose turn it was, because the tally has been
away with the player and everybody has a reason to prefer that it stay vague.

**Hebbel Marchen**, the reeve, wants the tally and wants it read aloud. That is the first beat:
she will not name anyone until she has it in her hand.

## The middle

The tally says Cairnhead's turn belonged to **Orin Vane**, who is not in Salt Wick and not on
Cairnhead either — he is at Emberfold, a day inland, where his sister keeps a mill.

At Cairnhead the player finds the beacon itself: a cairn of split wood, dry, never lit, and a
stack of new-cut timber beside it that nobody ordered. **Nesh Cairnhead** keeps the beacon path
and saw a boat put in below the headland the night it should have burned. She did not light it
because a man she did not know told her the rota had changed — and he had a tally of his own.

That is the turn: there are two tallies. Somebody has been cutting a second one.

Once the player has heard it, a courier comes up out of the valley and takes the tally off
them for "the reeve's own hand". He is lying. The player watches him go east with it, and the
story is now about getting to Emberfold before he does.

## The end

At Emberfold, **Orin Vane** is not hiding. He is the one who has been cutting the second
tally — not for raiders but because the rota as agreed put his own beacon on the two nights of
the year his mill has to run, and nobody would hear him about it. He has been quietly swapping
his nights with a man from up the coast who took payment and then took the smokehouse too.

The player can hand him back the tally he had taken from them, or keep it and carry the truth
to Salt Wick. Either way somebody's winter goes badly.

## Who is in it

| who | where | wants |
|---|---|---|
| Hebbel Marchen, reeve | Salt Wick, the square | the tally read aloud, and somebody to blame |
| Wend Sallow, smoker | Salt Wick, indoors at the smokehouse | to be believed about the boat |
| Nesh Cairnhead, beacon-keeper | Cairnhead, by the cairn | to have been right to doubt the rota |
| Orin Vane, miller | Emberfold, indoors at the mill | one night of the year back |
| Kell, courier | comes up the valley | the tally, for somebody else |

## What each place is for

- **Salt Wick** — the coast that gets raided. Where the story is set going, and where its
  consequences land. Full of people who all know something.
- **Cairnhead** — the evidence. A beacon that is obviously ready and obviously unlit, and one
  person who will say why.
- **Emberfold** — the reason. Inland, a journey off, and the only place the story can be
  finished rather than merely explained.

## What the cutscenes are for

Three, and each one shows something a conversation cannot.

1. **The watch turns out** — arriving at Salt Wick. The village walks in from three directions
   and gathers at the well before anybody speaks to the player. It says "this has already
   happened and they have already been arguing about it" in four seconds.
2. **The courier** — after Nesh explains the second tally. A man walks up out of the valley,
   takes the tally out of the player's hands, and leaves eastward with it. The player loses an
   object and gains a direction, and neither is announced by anybody.
3. **The beacon lights** — arriving at Emberfold with the tally gone. Behind the player,
   far off, Cairnhead finally burns. Somebody else lit it. Nothing is said.
